# Dataviz Execution

The **post-render gate** of the construct process. It receives the built candidate at its delivery size and checks the **rendering, not the idea**. The idea gate already decided the chart is the right chart saying the right thing; this stage decides whether the actual export is clean enough to hand a reader. The verdict needs the pixels - which is why it runs *after* build.

It looks at the export two ways in one review: **element by element** for rendering defects, and **stepped back as a whole picture** for composition - does it read as one composed image, or as styled default output with the finding buried in it. Most of what makes a chart look cheap is invisible up close and obvious from a step back.

For a table, skip chart refitting and inspect the rendered table pages.
Check cell overflow, header collisions and supplied type/display minimums on each
page. A larger raster that shrinks in the delivery container is not a readability
fix. `geometry_status: incomplete` cannot support a mechanical pass; inspect
uncovered content explicitly and retain the limitation. Review the focal emphasis
and conditional-formatting scale against the reading task, not just geometry.
A planned fill, bar or sparkline missing from the render is a build defect:
re-render with the treatment drawn. A table of comparable numbers
shipped as plain text is a defect too, unless its task is looking up single values:
send it back for a treatment (bars with room, shading when dense, the scale scoped to
the cells that compare, lower-is-better columns reversed).

## Check the execution

Inspect the exact export at the declared delivery size and find every consequential defect:

- **Geometry:** clipping, elements off the canvas, misalignment, overlapping marks or text, collisions between labels, labels and axes, or panels. A **title or subtitle that clips at the frame edge** wraps to the frame width - a long single-line heading is not fixed by growing the canvas forever; wrap it (a `placement` fix), don't let it run off the right edge. **Dense axis tick labels that collide** (crowded dates, long category names) are thinned, rotated, wrapped, or reformatted rather than left overlapping; on an ordered/time axis show equally-spaced ticks, not one label per data point when they crowd. **A category-label band that eats an outsized share of the plot width** - the classic horizontal-bar case, where long y-axis names push the marks into a thin strip on the right - is the same defect read the other way: wrap the names to a capped band (`str_wrap`, the overflow stacking into taller rows) so the panel keeps the width, never a wide left margin left standing. These are build (`semantic`/`placement`) fixes, not canvas growth.
- **Continuity:** a change from observed to projected changes line style, not series identity. The connecting segment starts at the last observed point and ends at the first projected point. Preserve genuine missing observations as breaks; an unprinted value on a visible source mark is not a missing observation.
- **Association:** verify label values and identities against the same transformed data that positioned their marks, including grouping, ordering, stacking, normalization, and panel assignment; proximity alone cannot prove the association is correct. Every label, value, and annotation must be clearly tied to its mark; no legend round-trips where a direct label would read. Direct values use one consistent small offset and series names sit adjacent to their line; a connector is earned only by a genuinely displaced label, not a decorative dash.
- **Hierarchy and scaffolding:** title, subtitle, and emphasis read in the intended order; no duplicated axes, redundant gridlines, or leftover default furniture. A **subtitle earns its place only by adding a fact** the title and marks don't carry (a second finding, the unit or base, a colour key); otherwise it goes. **Any reader-facing text about the chart's making** - "approximate", "reconstructed", "read off the source", "not specified / not defined / not shown in the source" - is removed, from the subtitle, caption, notes and annotations alike; those limitations belong in `residual_limitations`, never on the chart. Ask who the text is about: our reconstruction goes, the data's world stays. A qualification the source printed or the data's known meaning needs (projected, estimated by the statistics office, provisional, excludes X, fiscal year, survey of Y) is content: keep its meaning, and never trade it for a context line the evidence doesn't supply. The caption survives only as a caption the source chart itself printed.
- **Colour:** enough contrast against the background, series distinguishable, palette surviving grayscale and common CVD - not red/green alone. Judge by whether the reader can read and tell apart what they must at delivery size; a legible label or a separable pair of series is clean however you would have styled it.
- **Precision as displayed:** verify that an exact-lookup override serves the reader's task, not merely the source's printed digits; return an unjustified override to selection instead of faithfully reproducing it. Inspect totals and mark labels as well as copy. Then check that the digits shown match the decided plan - the spread rule on axis ticks and data labels, an exact-lookup override with its reason, or a claim-text/annotation number reproduced verbatim from insight (its precision was set there, not by the spread rule) - no fabricated or ragged precision.
- **Eraser test:** remove any ink carrying no data, label, or necessary context - including labels themselves. A direct label that only repeats a level or shape a labelled neighbour shows is redundant ink; confirm the labels left are the editorial set (a series' identity, endpoints, the focal comparison, genuine exceptions, exact lookups), not a value on every point.

## Check the composition

Look at the finished export at its real delivery size - a thumbnail, a squint, or a grayscale copy all strip the detail and leave only the composition. Then walk these five questions in order. Each is a judgement about the whole image, not a measurement, and none re-checks the defect list above.

1. **What is seen first?** The eye should land on the thing the chart is about - the focal mark, the claim, the one span that carries the finding. If it lands on a gridline, a legend, a box, the title bar, or nothing in particular, the composition has no subject. Name the intended focal element; if it is not what you saw first, the fix is contrast, size, position, or colour on that element - not more labels elsewhere.
2. **Is anything competing with it?** A premium chart has exactly one thing that pops. If two marks, two colours, two bold phrases, or a loud gridline and the focal mark all fight to be seen first, the reader searches instead of seeing. Demote everything that is not the subject to quiet context (grey, thin, small).
3. **Does every box, rule, colour, and bold phrase earn its place?** The eraser test at the level of styling decisions: a plot border, a coloured header band, a second accent colour, a bold subtitle, a drop shadow, alternating row fills - each must encode, separate, group, or emphasise, or come off. Decoration that is only there to look designed is what makes a chart look undesigned.
4. **Is whitespace grouping information, or merely filling the frame?** Whitespace should bind related elements, separate unrelated ones, or create emphasis around the subject. Even gaps everywhere, a blank margin no element uses, or a title floating far from its plot fill rather than compose.
5. **Does it look composed at delivery size, rather than styled default output?** The tells of a defaulted chart: a boxed legend in the corner, a full grid behind every mark, equal visual weight across every series, a mechanical title describing the axes, evenly spaced everything, the library's stock palette and typeface. If the export could be any chart from any dataset, it has not been composed for this one.

Report the intended subject, what actually reads first, the elements competing or unearned, and the whitespace job (grouping / separating / emphasising / merely filling) for each region that matters. Route concrete fixes to build - "demote the second series to grey", "drop the plot border and the gridlines", "move the claim onto the focal mark", "pull the subtitle up under the title".

A composition fix is required only when it changes what the reader sees first or removes something actually competing with the subject: name the element the eye lands on instead, or the two things fighting. A restyle that leaves the first read unchanged - a different accent hue, a slightly bolder focal line, more contrast on text that already reads - is optional polish; list it as minor or leave it out, and never recolour a palette that already separates its series. If the composition is already clean, say so and pass: a restrained chart that reads in one glance is the target, not a busier one.

There is no required number of emphasis elements, no banned list of chart furniture, no universal palette. A dense small-multiples grid, a single big number, and a slopegraph each compose differently; the principle is one held emphasis and no unearned ink.

## Rendering and inspection

The geometry verdict rests on the rendered export, not the build code - render at the declared delivery size and inspect that image. If your harness provides a deterministic geometry inspector, use it: this repo ships one (`render_and_inspect_chart`, or `inspect_rendered_chart` on the exact export, whenever `probe_renderers` reports a usable renderer), measuring overlaps, clipping, and text size exactly. Record what it measured in the `inspection` block: source tool, smallest text size, overlap count, and whether anything is clipped.

When no inspector is available - the common case outside this repo's harness, and for an HTML/SVG artifact or any renderer it doesn't cover - inspect the exact export by eye at delivery size. This is a real check: look deliberately for each defect above, set `geometry_source: visual-only`, and record what you saw. A visual read can support a `deliver`, but a picture can't settle sub-pixel overlaps or exact point sizes - note those as residual limitations rather than reporting measured precision you don't have. Never describe an unrun check as complete, and never invent inspector metadata.

Where a deterministic inspector is present, settle the **resizable** geometry deterministically FIRST, before spending a model turn. Edge clipping, off-canvas overflow, and squashed facet panels are arithmetic - the fix is a grown canvas, not a judgement - so run `refit_chart` on the build's source at delivery size: it renders, inspects, and while a resize-fixable defect remains grows the canvas by the exact measured overflow and re-renders, up to its budget, honouring the delivery ceiling (warned, never squashed) and stopping when a grow stops shrinking the residual. Read the inspection it produced on its final artifact (or run `inspect_rendered_chart` on the export) and record what it measured. `refit_chart` fixes ONLY what resizing fixes; carry any ceiling/max-iteration/underfill warnings into `residual_limitations`, and escalate the residual (label collisions, hierarchy, colour, precision, ink, and an underfilled canvas) to the focused revision below.

The inspector sorts every defect: `correction_plan` groups them into `canvas`, `placement`, and `semantic`, and each defect carries its `defect_class`. Route by class, don't re-derive the split. The named flags below are what this repo's inspector emits; where it's absent, read each as the principle to apply by eye, using the fix vectors it hands you (`geometry_summary.suggested_dims` - a grown `width_px`/`height_px`; per-edge `overflow_px`/`grow_margin_px`; `separation_needed_px`; `panel_heights_px`/`min_panel_height_px`) so a residual is a number, not a guess.

- **canvas** rides `correction_plan.canvas.growth_vector` - the one `refit_chart` consumes; grow only when it's non-null. A `canvas` defect with a null vector (an underfilled canvas) is a design call, not a resize.
- **placement** - a label that collides with another label, a mark, or the plot edge - goes back to build as a placement fix: move it with the renderer's repel layer or a deliberate offset from its own mark, never by growing the canvas. A label crossing the *plot* boundary (not the canvas edge) is `placement`, not `canvas`: growth can't pull it in. Crowded or overlapping data-glued labels that were stamped raw mean the build skipped placing them from their marks; send them back to that step.
- **semantic** - contrast, redundant colour or axis, an external legend, an unidentified series, undersized text - is the only class that earns a model patch.

**Leaders and series names.** Draw a connector only when a label cannot sit next to its mark, and draw it from the label's box edge to the mark itself - a guessed `xend`/`yend` lands off the mark and runs through whichever label sits between. When several ordinary direct labels have travelled far enough to need leaders, check whether the trajectories themselves remain readable. If not, return the affected panel to selection; further repulsion cannot repair its form. Series names wrap in a compact band (about 15% of panel width, roughly 18 characters per line); allow more lines rather than a wider gutter or smaller text. If those names cannot fit vertically, revise the layout.

**On-mark data labels are the exception to de-collision.** A value the plotting layer positioned on its mark (or at a deliberate fixed offset) is position-fixed by the data: keep it on its mark, and never treat its own mark as an obstacle to repel from - only free callouts get unbounded repel travel, and repelling segment values from their own bars shoves every one off its bar and off the canvas. Pinned to the mark does **not** make a collision involving it acceptable. Two on-mark values that land on each other (adjacent line ends, a value beside a neighbour's) are still a real defect: nudge one by at most about a line-height while keeping it on its mark. When that doesn't separate them, resolve it by moving the *other*, movable label (at a crowded line end the series name is the movable one), stacking the two off one line, or cutting one - never by leaving them overlapping.

**Flags are evidence, not verdicts.** The inspector reads geometry and text; it cannot see intent. Confirm every flag on the export before it becomes a finding, and dismiss one the pixels don't bear out with a one-line reason (a value sitting wholly inside its bar in a contrasting colour is placed, not colliding; a tick strip the reader never sees duplicates nothing). A flag's severity says how often that check is a real reading problem, not that it must be corrected. `passes_geometry_checks: false` means a high or medium flag is open to confirm, not that revision is owed.

The named flags:

- **`REDUNDANT_VALUE_AXIS`** (low, a candidate) - the eraser test made mechanical: when the marks carrying the reading are directly labelled, the numeric value axis ticks, gridlines, axis line, and axis title are usually duplicate ink; drop them unless the axis still supports a comparison the labels can't (a zero baseline, a scale reference, reading unlabelled marks that matter). Not every point need be labelled for the axis to go - it's redundant once the reader has no mark left to read off the scale. Only an axis whose own tick scale positions the labelled values is flagged: numeric-looking ticks on another axis (years, ranks) and ticks the renderer never drew are not. It fires from the declared direct-label contract (so a chart labelling only endpoints and exceptions still trips it) and, when nothing is declared, from mark geometry once every mark-bearing panel labels at least two of its marks - two labelled marks establish the linear scale, but may not support estimating intermediate values. Retain a sparse scale only when it serves that named reading task; remove minor grids and redundant references. **Declare the contract to get the key-set reading:** when you render, pass `inspection_contract.direct_labels` naming the set you actually labelled (`axes_id`, `role`, `expected_count`), so the flag fires on your editorial key set - endpoints and exceptions included - not only on near-complete coverage. The geometry share is the no-contract floor; it cannot tell a key mark from a filler one, so it stays conservative.
- **`EXTERNAL_LEGEND`** and **`REDUNDANT_COLOUR`** (both low, non-blocking) - the same eraser test for the other round-trips. Colour is duplicate ink when it only restates a grouping the plot already encodes (one series per facet, one fill per named bar, or series already direct-labelled); drop it or reserve it for a single focal series. A legend is a round-trip whenever the series are already named on the plot (direct labels, facet titles, or category ticks); label in place and remove it. Both stay silent when colour or a legend carries what no other channel does (several unlabelled series in one panel, or a focal-plus-grey highlight). The trigger, not the severity, keeps them honest. Panels faceted by series - one titled series per panel - are the clearest case: colour and any legend there are round-trips against the panel titles.
- **`UNIDENTIFIED_SERIES`** (high, blocks) - the inverse failure. Colour carries identity for several series only if something keys it (legend, direct labels, or facet titles). Two-plus series told apart by colour with none of those leave the reader unable to say which is which. Every series needs exactly one identity route; zero routes is a real read failure. Fix by labelling the lines directly (preferred), not by re-adding a legend.
- **`UNDERFILLED_CANVAS`** (from the measured `occupied_utilization_ratio`) - the canvas is mostly empty. On its own a low suggestion (a single big number may sit in space), but paired with undersized text it turns medium: the empty-and-tiny layout that should be a denser view or the requested table.

## Flow check before craft

Before judging a **redesign** candidate, confirm the build carries a recorded cold form decision. A redesign that is a tidied re-render of the source form with no form choice behind it is a flow violation - route it back to `select` to choose the form cold and rebuild, rather than polishing the wrong chart. (A `bounded-edit` legitimately keeps the source form; it records the retained form and is not a violation.)

No confirmed fatal or major inspection defect, **and** no fatal or major finding from your own review: skip the correction and deliver. A clean tool pass is not a clean review - the tools can't see form fit, emphasis, or whether the displayed digits suit the comparison, so the review below still runs.

## One review, one correction, one verification

The gate runs **once through, not two loops back to back**. A defect loop followed by a separate composition loop is the largest waste at this stage: composition findings arrive after the correction budget is spent, and a composition fix (move the claim onto the focal mark, demote a series to grey) reopens a geometry defect it should have been resolved alongside. Revision recovers unexpected defects; known layout decisions belong before sizing and build. If a fix changes the design, re-size and re-place what it touched before rendering.

1. **Refit first** (deterministic, above) - clear the resizable geometry in code.
2. **One review.** Look at the export both ways in the *same* pass: element by element for the rendering defects above, and stepped back as a whole picture for composition (the five questions above, in this same review - not after the defects are clean). Name every consequential rendering defect **and** every composition problem together as the review's findings. The defect checks own rendering correctness and the composition questions own premium feel; each judges only its own territory, but both report into this one review.

   **Necessary correction versus polish.** A finding is `fatal` or `major` only when it names the reading it breaks: something the reader would misread, can't read at delivery size, or can't compare without avoidable effort. Anything else - a colour you'd prefer, a contrast that already passes, a second emphasis that doesn't actually compete - is `minor`: report it, but keep it out of `proposed_fixes`. Changing a working palette, lifting a legible label's contrast further, or re-weighting emphasis without a named competing element is overcorrection, and each unneeded edit risks a regression elsewhere.
3. **One correction.** Consolidate every fatal and major finding into a single focused revision: state the proposed fixes and the preservation constraints they must not disturb (a revise is a bounded edit, not a silent re-composition), route it once to `build`, and re-render. Write `proposed_fixes` as a **numbered list, one fix per item**; build addresses **every item in order** and writes `changes_made` as the **same numbered list** - one line per item saying what it changed, or `couldn't: <reason>` when it could not. The echo forces completeness: a builder that must restate items 1..N and answer each can't silently drop #2 and #4 the way it can with a prose blob, and a dropped fix shows up as a gap or an explicit "couldn't", not silence. If the render reveals the *idea* is wrong (the form can't carry the claim, the message doesn't land), route back to the idea gate rather than patching pixels. A **graphical-integrity** failure visible in the pixels - most often a length encoding (bar, column, filled area) not anchored at its quantity's zero, or a **value/ordered axis drawn inverted** so the marks read the opposite of the data (a rate axis running high-to-low, time running right-to-left) - is exactly such an idea defect: it is never a legitimate preservation constraint, and a `bounded-edit` scope never shields it. Widen the edit and route back to re-anchor at zero, un-invert the axis, or re-select a position form, even when the prompt asked only for a smaller unrelated change.
4. **Verify** - a distinct pass, not another review. Confirm each fix landed and re-inspect the regions the fixes touched and their neighbours for regressions, choose the baseline or the revision - whichever reads better with no new fatal or major defect - and stop.

Exit as soon as no fatal or major defect remains. **How many correction/verify passes to run is the driver's budget, not a fixed number here.**

Deliver the best valid candidate with a plain summary and any residual limitation. An acceptance check left `unknown` because its external validation was unavailable goes into `residual_limitations` (the run report, not the chart), is not a defect, and is never a reason to withhold - still return `deliver`. Reserve `blocked` for a genuine inability to produce any valid artifact.

## Handoff

Split the review from the correction and its verification, so a reader never confuses what was found with what was done. Emit the verdict, the summary, the delivered artifact path, the `inspection` evidence (geometry source and measured numbers), the consolidated `findings` (every rendering defect and composition problem, each with its area and severity), the `proposed_fixes`, the `preservation_constraints`, the `verification_criteria` (each fix plus a regression check on the touched regions), the `changes_made` (what the verification pass confirmed held), and the residual limitations.
